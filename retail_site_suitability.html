Retail Site Suitability Dashboard

Tempe, Arizona

This project evaluates candidate retail parcels in Tempe using a weighted GIS suitability model. Parcel characteristics, zoning and land use, development status, population, and measured proximity to roads, bus stops, and light rail were converted into standardized scores and combined into a Retail Suitability Index. The final results were published to an interactive ArcGIS Dashboard for parcel-by-parcel comparison.

Project Goal

The goal was to screen and rank candidate parcels for relative retail suitability using a repeatable parcel-level scoring model. Rather than relying on a single factor, the analysis compared each parcel using physical size, zoning, land use, development status, surrounding population, and transportation accessibility.

GIS Methods

  • Prepared a candidate parcel layer with parcel area, zoning, land use, development status, and population attributes.
  • Calculated parcel proximity in feet to Valley Metro bus stops, major roads, and light rail.
  • Converted eight suitability factors into standardized 1–5 score fields: size, zoning, bus access, road access, light rail access, land use, development status, and population.
  • Calculated a weighted Retail Suitability Index: 5% size, 15% zoning, 10% bus access, 15% road access, 5% light rail access, 20% land use, 15% development status, and 15% population.
  • Classified final index values into Low, Moderate, High, Very High, and Excellent suitability classes.
  • Published the scored candidate parcels as a hosted web layer and configured an ArcGIS Dashboard for interactive site comparison.

Data Sources

  • City of Tempe GIS / Open Data
  • Maricopa County parcel and reference data
  • Valley Metro transit data
  • U.S. Census Bureau ACS population and TIGER/Line geography
  • Esri basemap and ArcGIS Online web mapping tools

Key Takeaway

The project demonstrates a complete suitability workflow: assembling parcel-level variables, measuring accessibility, standardizing different criteria to a common 1–5 scale, applying explicit weights, and converting the final index into clear suitability classes. The result is a transparent screening model that can be inspected parcel by parcel rather than a map based on subjective visual judgment.

Limitations

This dashboard is a planning-focused screening tool, not a final site selection model. Suitability results could change with additional variables such as lease cost, ownership status, traffic counts, zoning restrictions, market demand, or business-specific requirements.
